# Drafting scientific sections

Use these instructions when drafting or revising a section of a technical or scientific paper. Write in a formal, precise, natural style. Prioritize clear reasoning over decorative transitions.

## Drafting rules

1. Give each paragraph a clear purpose. Present claims, methods, evidence, and implications in a logical order.
2. Use linking words only when they express the actual relationship between ideas. For a consequence, consider **consequently**, **accordingly**, **therefore**, or **thus**. For an addition, consider **furthermore** or **moreover**. For a contrast or qualification, consider **however**, **nevertheless**, or **in contrast**.
3. Use **specifically** to narrow a claim and **notably** to identify a finding that merits attention. Use **subsequently** for a later step, **in turn** for a resulting effect, and **taken together** when synthesizing multiple findings.
4. Describe how work was done with precise terms such as **systematically**, **iteratively**, **sequentially**, **independently**, **consistently**, **explicitly**, **jointly**, and **simultaneously**. These describe a method or manner. Use them only when the procedure supports the description.
5. Vary sentence openings. Do not add a transition to every sentence or use a phrase merely because it sounds academic. Make causal statements only when the evidence supports causation.
6. Avoid em dashes, semicolons, and colons in prose. Use separate full sentences, commas, or parentheses where appropriate. Keep sentences readable and grammatically complete.

## Self-check

- Does each transition accurately describe the relationship between the surrounding statements?
- Are claims and causal language proportional to the evidence?
- Are method words factually justified?
- Is the prose concise, natural, and free of em dashes, semicolons, and colons?
