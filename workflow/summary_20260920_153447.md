# Workflow summary — penalized convergence, equilibrium audits, and reinitialization

Created: 2026-09-20 15:34:47 Europe/Vienna

## Executive conclusion

We followed seven regional update orders for up to 40 penalized, damped Gauss--Seidel sweeps. Only three of the seven strategy paths met the declared movement criterion. Importantly, **none of those three converged strategy profiles passed an independent zero-proximal equilibrium audit**. This confirms that small changes in the penalized algorithm are evidence of numerical stabilization, not evidence of equilibrium.

Controlled zero-proximal reinitialization around the three converged profiles was much more successful. It produced **14 profiles satisfying the one-start local 1% criterion**: six from the previously converged CH-first profile and four from each of the newly converged AF-first and EU-first profiles. These are locally observable computational candidates, not globally certified Nash equilibria.

For the paper, avoid the labels “Stage 1” and “Stage 2.” The clearer terminology is **penalized and damped basin discovery**, followed by **zero-proximal reinitialization and frozen-profile regret verification**.

## 1. Penalized and damped sweeps

The seven update orders were originally run for 30 sweeps. The six paths that had not previously met the movement criterion were restarted from their exact sweep-30 strategies and sweep-30 penalty states and continued for ten more sweeps. The same corrected model, terminal salvage fraction of 0.5, adaptive penalty logic, and damped Gauss--Seidel procedure were retained.

The resulting classification after at most 40 sweeps was:

| Update order | Movement result | Relevant value |
|---|---|---:|
| CH–AF–APAC–EU–ROW–US | converged earlier | 0.498% at sweep 26 |
| AF–EU–US–APAC–ROW–CH | converged | first clean pass at sweep 33; 0.191% at sweep 40 |
| EU–US–AF–ROW–APAC–CH | converged | first clean pass at sweep 33; 0.191% at sweep 40 |
| CH–AF–EU–US–ROW–APAC | did not converge | 8.381% at sweep 40; solve-quality caveat |
| CH–ROW–APAC–US–EU–AF | did not converge | 32.937% at sweep 40 |
| US–APAC–AF–ROW–EU–CH | did not converge | 2.045% at sweep 40 |
| US–ROW–EU–APAC–AF–CH | did not converge | 2.043% at sweep 40 |

Thus, the headline result is **3 of 7 movement-converged strategy paths**. The other four did not become stable merely by adding ten sweeps; one China-first path became substantially less stable.

## 2. Direct equilibrium audits

Each of the three movement-converged profiles was checked through independent, zero-proximal unilateral best responses against one common frozen profile. All reported best-response solves succeeded, but none met the 1% maximum relative-gain criterion:

| Penalized-converged profile | Maximum unilateral gain | Limiting player | Result |
|---|---:|---|---|
| CH–AF–APAC–EU–ROW–US | 16.037% | APAC | fails |
| AF–EU–US–APAC–ROW–CH | 11.088% | APAC | fails |
| EU–US–AF–ROW–APAC–CH | 10.445% | APAC | fails |

The CH-first value comes from the earlier three-start audit; the two new values come from the requested one-start audits. Despite that difference in audit depth, the conclusion is unambiguous in all three cases: **strategy convergence under penalties is not equilibrium convergence in the original economic game**.

## 3. Zero-proximal reinitialization

Because the direct profiles failed, we searched nearby attraction basins without algorithmic proximal penalties. For each converged anchor, the grid varied:

- bilateral export offers initialized at 1.00 or 1.20 times period-specific manufacturing cost;
- the anchor capacity-change path weighted by 0.50 or 1.00;
- fixed damping of 0.30 or 0.40.

This gives eight branches per anchor. Each branch used zero proximal penalties, no move cap, no gain filter, no player freezing, and at most 15 sweeps. The initialization and every completed sweep were audited against a common frozen profile. A profile was accepted only when all six one-start best responses succeeded and the maximum normalized unilateral gain was at most 1%.

Results tied specifically to the three penalized-converged anchors were:

| Anchor order | Accepted branches | Accepted gain range |
|---|---:|---:|
| CH–AF–APAC–EU–ROW–US | 6 of 8 | 0.518%–0.961% |
| AF–EU–US–APAC–ROW–CH | 4 of 8 | 0.786%–0.975% |
| EU–US–AF–ROW–APAC–CH | 4 of 8 | 0.809%–0.895% |
| **Total** | **14 of 24** | **all at or below 1%** |

The older `outputs/15_equilibria` package contains 15 one-start candidates across three earlier China-first anchors: six from CH–AF–APAC–EU–ROW–US and nine from two anchors that did not ultimately satisfy the movement criterion. That historical total should not be confused with the cleaner 14-candidate set derived only from the three penalized-converged anchors.

The six older CH-first candidates were subsequently subjected to a three-start audit and none remained below 1%. The eight new AF-first and EU-first candidates have so far received only the one-start audit. Consequently, the present 14 profiles support a **local multiplicity** claim under the declared one-start search, not multistart robustness or global equilibrium certification.

## 4. Economic interpretation

The reinitialized profiles do not all collapse to one economic outcome:

- The AF-first family tends toward slightly lower capacity and higher prices, particularly in the import-dependent regions.
- Three of the four EU-first profiles are relatively close to matched older CH-first outcomes.
- The EU-first `pf120_k100_a040` profile reaches a distinctly high-capacity, low-price basin. Relative to the matched older branch, 2040 capacity rises from about 868 GW to 1,246 GW while the mean regional 2040 price falls from about $150/kW to $118/kW. This is a more competition-like outcome, but not necessarily a more efficient one: capacity is roughly 70% above global demand, and several importing regions still lose welfare relative to the planner benchmark.
- Cross-border trade totals remain comparatively stable; capacity and price differences provide the clearer evidence of economically distinct basins.

Damping is an algorithmic rather than an economic parameter. Accepted profiles differing only in damping should therefore not automatically be counted as separate economic equilibria. Their capacity and price vectors are not identical, but bilateral flow differences are larger and may partly reflect alternative trade allocations. A final paper presentation should cluster candidates using economically meaningful capacity and price distances and report representative basin families.

## 5. What this means methodologically

The results support the following narrative:

1. Penalization and damping can stabilize the sequential algorithm and locate promising regions of strategy space.
2. Movement convergence by itself is not an equilibrium test; all three stabilized endpoints retained unilateral gains above 10% when audited directly.
3. Removing penalties and deliberately varying price, capacity, damping, and update-order initializations exposes multiple local attraction basins.
4. Frozen-profile zero-proximal regret tests provide the acceptance rule independently of the search dynamics.
5. The defensible result is evidence of **computationally observable local equilibrium multiplicity**, with an explicit one-start limitation—not proof of a unique or globally verified equilibrium.
