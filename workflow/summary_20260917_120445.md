# Equilibrium diagnostics, horizon treatment, and the salvage-value question

Created: 2026-09-17 12:04:45 Europe/Vienna

## Status

Three corrected-demand profiles (E1-E3) are accepted as one-start local
1%-equilibria. This session compared them, diagnosed why the manuscript's
reported profile is not an equilibrium, identified a horizon inconsistency
between the MPEC and the planner, and opened a serious question about whether
E2 and E3 survive a multistart audit. No model run was launched.

## Key findings

**Why the reported profile fails.** Proximal coefficients ramp to their maximum
(q=2, p=3, a=2, dK=2) by sweep 11 and omega decays to 0.4, while convergence is
declared on `Delta theta < 1e-2` for three sweeps. The paper's runs "converged"
at iterations 20-25, when the regularization was strongest. `Delta theta -> 0`
was therefore manufactured by the penalty pinning each update near
`theta_last`; it carries no information about unexploited unilateral gains.
This is the standard result that fixed points of a regularized iteration are not
fixed points of the original game, and it explains ROW's 47.58% audited gain.

**Methodological framing to adopt.** Present the method as penalty continuation
in two stages with two distinct tests. Stage 1 (c_pen > 0) is basin finding and
produces candidate anchors, explicitly not equilibria; `Delta theta` is a
movement diagnostic only. Stage 2 (c_pen = 0) releases the anchor under fixed
damping in the original economic game and audits every sweep by freezing the
profile and solving all six unilateral best responses at zero penalty. The
acceptance epsilon is exactly the residual regularization bias. The 28-branch
direct zero-proximal grid is the control: 1 acceptance in 28 branches with an
80-sweep cap and ~50 CPU-hours, versus acceptance in 5-7 sweeps from Stage-1
anchors. Reframe the contribution as cheap basin finding plus verification in the
unregularized game, not as "better convergence".

**Demand never binds.** Served demand equals Dmax in every region, period and
equilibrium (deviation ~1e-8), because WTP intercepts (1100-2400 $/kW) sit far
above equilibrium prices (86-292 $/kW). The elasticity calibration affects only
consumer-surplus accounting, not quantities. Multiplicity is therefore entirely
supply-side, and strategic pricing is close to a pure transfer: global welfare
spreads only 0.12% across E1-E3 while APAC's payoff spreads 7.6% and China's
2.9%.

**Horizon asymmetry (bug-level).** The MPEC objective sums over the full set T
including 2045; the planner objective sums over T_plan and excludes 2045. The
regional objective values stored in the audits therefore include a 2045 term and
are not on the manuscript's 2025-2040 reporting basis. Any EPEC-versus-planner
gap and any welfare-based selection must be recomputed on a common period set.
The 0.12% spread is small enough that the selection rule could flip.

**Period 1 determines the equilibrium.** China's 2025 delivered offer against
each region's own manufacturing cost decides who exits: EU (299), US (321) and
ROW (353) are undercut in all three equilibria and all exit; Africa (181) is
never undercut and retains its 2-4 GW everywhere. APAC is the knife-edge, and
the decisive margin is in the export markets. Against APAC's delivered cost
floor into the EU (282.3), China offers 271.1 in E1 (undercut by 11.2, APAC
collapses 110 -> 27 GW) but 285.4 in E2 and 291.5 in E3 (margin +3.1 and +9.2,
APAC expands to 146 and 161 GW). One first-period pricing decision selects the
equilibrium.

**Capacity outcome is a cliff, not a glide.** Global capacity falls 35-42% in the
first period and is then nearly flat: 1401 -> 810/906/901 -> 802/919/931 ->
757/783/748 GW for E1/E2/E3. The manuscript's gradual 1401 -> 1290 GW narrative
does not survive the demand correction. EU/US exit is robust in direction; E2
retains a 1.9 GW / 3.5 GW sliver at full utilization.

**ROW holds a real option, not idle waste.** In E1, ROW holds ~99 GW at 0%
utilization through 2030 and 2035, then runs the entire stock at 100% in 2040
(89.0 domestic, 5.9 to APAC, 4.0 to EU). The trigger is clean: lambda_ROW sits
below ROW's manufacturing cost in every idle period and crosses above it exactly
when ROW starts producing. The option has value because the model is
asymmetric - decommissioning is capped at 100% of stock per year while expansion
is an absolute rate cap (ROW 4.33 GW/yr, EU 2.67, APAC 12.67), so exit is
quasi-irreversible. In E2/E3 the option is held but never exercised, and its
return is disciplinary: lambda_ROW stays below ROW's cost in every period.

## Open concern: E2 and E3 may not be equilibria

At E3 in 2040, China's delivered offer exceeds APAC's delivered cost floor by
42.0 $/kW (EU) and 51.9 $/kW (US), yet APAC ships zero because it decommissioned
from 196 to 13.5 GW. Retaining that capacity is feasible (`dK[2035] = 0`, inside
both limits) and appears to be worth several percent of APAC's objective
(1,250,262 M$) from immediate production margin plus replacing 112.5 GW of
imports priced at 207.9 with domestic supply at 140.9. The audit reports APAC's
maximum gain as 0.911%. APAC is also the binding player in both E2 (0.836%) and
E3 (0.911%) - the two equilibria in which it expands - while in E1, where it is
crushed early, its gain is 0.037%.

This is a back-of-envelope from the stored profiles; duals are not in the
artifacts and `mu_offer` is subtracted from the producer term, so it needs the
actual solve. **Highest-priority computation: re-run APAC's zero-proximal best
response at the frozen E3 profile from a start in which APAC retains its 2035
capacity through 2040-2045.** If the gain exceeds 1%, E2 and E3 both fall and the
defensible set reduces to E1 plus the 0.9965% direct-grid profile.

## Salvage value: to investigate

Reviewer 2 asks about salvage when asset life exceeds the horizon. Facts
established this session:

- `terminal_capacity_value` in the code does **not** implement salvage. It sums
  over all T and rewards `Icap_pos`, i.e. it is a per-period investment subsidy.
  All four capacity levers default to 0.0 in RunConfig, so the statement that no
  salvage term is active is accurate as run.
- A proper term is `S_r = beta_terminal * v_r * Kcap[r,2045]`, linear, exogenous
  and smooth, adding no non-convexity. Discounting should use the end of the
  2045 block (2050, beta = 1.02^-25 = 0.6095), not beta_2045 = 0.6730.
- `v_r` is not uniform across the stock because the model has no retirement;
  `K[t+1] = K[t] + y_t*dK[t]` has no decay term. Either set `v_r = phi*c_inv_r`
  with phi age-aware (if plant life L <= 20 the 2025 vintage contributes zero
  and the problem disposes of itself), or add explicit retirement
  `K[t+1] = (1-delta*y_t)*K[t] + y_t*dK[t]`.
- At r_d = 0.02 and phi = 0.5, per GW discounted: EU credit 58.9 vs holding cost
  138.2 (still exits); ROW 42.1 vs 99.0 (retention 43% cheaper); APAC 19.3 vs
  20.5 over 2040+2045 (a coin flip). Salvage is therefore precisely targeted at
  the ambiguous APAC result and materially contaminates the ROW result.

**Argument against adopting it.** The model's existing holding incentive is
*option* value, which decays as the horizon runs out, not *asset* value, which
does not. Extending the horizon (adding 2050) restores the truncated option
value and is the better-matched fix; salvage rewards stock-holding per se and
hands a referee the reply that ROW retained capacity because it was worth
something. Annuitizing capex via a capital recovery charge removes the terminal
distortion entirely with no phi to defend, but is a next-paper change.

**Cost either way.** Any nonzero phi invalidates E1-E3 and requires a fresh
Stage 1 + Stage 2 search. The proposed compromise for this revision is to ship at
phi = 0 with the terminal period scoped as a reporting buffer, and to run the
salvage sensitivity on the planner only - a single QCP that must be re-solved on
the corrected workbook regardless.

## Other manuscript defects confirmed

- The reported profile is not an equilibrium (ROW 47.58%; CH, APAC, US, AF also
  above 1%).
- All three historical "converged" runs used b_dem slopes inconsistent with the
  workbook formula (23 of 24 coefficients wrong).
- Swanson's law is implemented as an **exogenous** schedule off IEA NZE global
  cumulative capacity (`data_prep.py`), not endogenous to model shipments; the
  "Initial point" subsection and the revision response both describe it as
  endogenous.
- The implemented producer term subtracts the capacity scarcity rent
  `mu_offer`, which paper eq. (7) does not show.
- Stop using "converged" for E1-E3; they are accepted profiles verified to 1%,
  and acceptance occurred before sustained fixed-point convergence.
