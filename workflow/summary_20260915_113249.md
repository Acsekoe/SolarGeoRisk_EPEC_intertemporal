## Agreed methodological direction

- The revision will present multiple computational equilibrium profiles and discuss their common features and differences. It will not claim uniqueness or global optimality.
- Equilibrium verification uses one common frozen candidate profile, zero algorithmic proximal penalties, no damping, and one local solve per player's unilateral deviation problem initialized from the candidate strategy.
- A candidate passes when every solve succeeds and the maximum nonnegative relative payoff improvement is at most 1%.
- The manuscript should call this a **computational local 1%-equilibrium criterion**, not a proof of a global Nash equilibrium.
- Fanzeres, Street, and Pozo (2020) support the fixed-rivals best-response-oracle logic. Zhang et al. (2025) support interpreting fixed-rivals payoff improvements as an epsilon/no-regret measure. Both references are now cited in the tracked manuscript.
