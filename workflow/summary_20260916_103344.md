# Methodological status: zero-proximal equilibrium search

## Central problem

The paper contains three Gauss--Seidel endpoint profiles, one for each manuscript player order, whose reported strategy-change stopping criterion was satisfied. These profiles are worth investigating because they are stable outputs of the penalized/damped algorithm. They are not, by that fact alone, equilibria of the original economic game: equilibrium still requires a common-frozen-profile unilateral-deviation audit with all algorithmic proximal penalties set to zero.

The defensible research question is therefore:

> Starting from the three stable manuscript candidates, does an ordinary, predeclared, zero-proximal Gauss--Seidel release procedure reach a profile satisfying the local 1% unilateral-deviation criterion?

The methodological difficulty is selection after observing results. Resetting selected variables, trying interpolations toward manufacturing cost, freezing players already below the tolerance, imposing small move caps, and changing damping after inspecting trajectories can all be useful exploratory tools. However, if these choices are made adaptively, it becomes difficult to distinguish a reproducible algorithm from hand-tuned equilibrium hunting. A successful frozen audit validates the final profile locally, but it does not by itself make an exploratory search path a clean algorithmic comparison.

## Overnight direct-search control

The completed control run is:

`outputs/demand_calibration/direct_zero_prox_grid_20260915_232405/`

It used corrected demand and no Stage-1 profile or source workbook:

- 7 Gauss--Seidel player orders;
- low and high price starts at 0.90 and 1.20 times manufacturing cost;
- fixed damping `alpha = 0.40` and `alpha = 0.70`;
- zero algorithmic proximal penalties;
- no move caps and no player freezing;
- one optimizer start per best response;
- a maximum of 80 complete sweeps;
- a common-frozen-profile, one-start, zero-proximal audit after every sweep.

All 28 branches reached a terminal state:

- **1 accepted local 1% candidate**;
- **17 exhausted 80 sweeps without passing**;
- **10 terminated when a one-start best-response solve failed**.

The sole accepted direct candidate is:

- order `AF-EU-US-APAC-ROW-CH`;
- high-price start, factor `1.20`;
- `alpha = 0.40`;
- sweep 10;
- maximum frozen relative unilateral gain `0.996503%`, APAC;
- all six frozen deviation solves successful.

This is a boundary-close local computational 1%-equilibrium. The trajectory did not establish sustained fixed-point convergence before acceptance, so the precise statement is that the branch **produced an accepted intermediate candidate**, not that ordinary Gauss--Seidel converged asymptotically.

The lower damping was materially better in this grid: `alpha = 0.40` had a median best frozen gain of `2.665%` and median final strategy change of `0.01217`, compared with `4.248%` and `0.04765` for `alpha = 0.70`. No stable period-2 through period-8 whole-profile cycle was detected. Seven branches showed a downward trend consistent with slow convergence; most others exhibited irregular best-response branch changes. Tail movements were dominated mainly by APAC capacity in 13 branches, CH capacity in 9, and ROW capacity in 5.

## What was actually done to obtain the earlier corrected-demand candidates

Four accepted profiles came from the exploratory diversified release search:

| Order | Release branch | Transformation from Stage 1 | Accepted sweep | Frozen maximum gain |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| CH-AF-APAC-EU-ROW-US | `cost_staged` | Retain Stage-1 capacities; reset offers to cost | 13 | 0.8770% APAC |
| CH-AF-EU-US-ROW-APAC | `cost_staged` | Retain Stage-1 capacities; reset offers to cost | 16 | 0.7641% APAC |
| CH-ROW-APAC-US-EU-AF | `cost_staged` | Retain Stage-1 capacities; reset offers to cost | 20 | 0.9927% APAC |
| CH-AF-APAC-EU-ROW-US | `halfcost_selective` | Retain Stage-1 capacities; move offers 50% toward cost | 29 | 0.8255% APAC |

These transformations and updates were scripted and reproducible, not literal manual edits. Nevertheless, the branch definitions were developed exploratorily. They skipped players whose sequential gain was already at or below 1%, used normalized move caps, and eventually used `omega = 0.10`; the caps could reduce the effective update weight further. This conflicts with the later methodological rule that damping should not fall below 0.30. The four final frozen audits remain valid under the agreed local criterion, but these profiles should not be the primary evidence for the effectiveness of ordinary Gauss--Seidel or for a pre-specified proximal-release algorithm.

A cleaner Stage-1-assisted result also exists:

- corrected Stage-1 capacities;
- prices reset to manufacturing cost;
- fixed `alpha = 0.30`;
- zero proximal penalties;
- no move cap or player freezing;
- accepted at sweep 6 with maximum gain `0.621395%`, CH.

This is methodologically stronger than the four exploratory profiles and is currently the strongest corrected-demand candidate. However, its price reset is still a basin transformation. It therefore does not fully answer whether the exact three stable manuscript profiles lead directly to zero-proximal equilibria.

## Recommended confirmatory experiment

The next experiment should be fixed before seeing its outcomes and should start from the **exact three stable manuscript candidates**:

1. Lock the candidate iteration and hash for each of the three manuscript orders.
2. Do not reset or interpolate capacities, offer prices, or other strategic variables.
3. Set every algorithmic proximal coefficient to zero from the first released sweep.
4. Update every player in every sweep; do not freeze players selectively.
5. Use no move cap.
6. Use fixed damping values declared in advance, preferably `alpha in {0.30, 0.40, 0.70}`. This gives 3 candidates times 3 damping values = 9 branches.
7. Run at most 80 complete sweeps for every branch.
8. Audit the unchanged starting profile at sweep 0 and every subsequent common profile using the agreed one-start, common-frozen-profile, zero-proximal deviation audit.
9. Accept the first sweep with all six solves successful and maximum relative gain at or below 1%.
10. Preserve and report every branch, including failures and non-passes; do not add transformations after seeing the results.

This experiment directly tests whether the three stable outputs of the paper algorithm are useful equilibrium candidates once the artificial stabilization is removed. If one or more pass, the paper can credibly state that the proximal algorithm generates basins from which the original game admits locally verified 1%-equilibria. If none pass, the honest conclusion is that the stable penalized endpoints are not themselves sufficient and that the successful cost-price transformations belong to an exploratory equilibrium-search layer.

A separate matched experiment can later isolate the value of Stage-1 capacities by comparing primitive versus Stage-1 capacity initializations under the same cost-price start, damping, player order, and zero-proximal update rule. The overnight 1-of-28 control is strong supportive evidence, but it is not perfectly matched to the existing Stage-1-assisted searches.

## Recommended paper framing now

- Treat the 28-branch direct grid as the unregularized control: only one boundary-close local candidate was found.
- Treat the three stable manuscript endpoints as a pre-specified candidate set to be tested by the 9-branch exact-profile release experiment.
- Use the `0.621395%` Stage-1-assisted candidate as the strongest current corrected-demand profile.
- Report the four exploratory candidates as robustness and multiplicity evidence, with their search protocol disclosed, rather than as the main causal proof that proximal penalties improve convergence.
- State that proximal penalties appear useful for stabilization and basin finding, but do not claim that they are mathematically necessary or that any audit is a global Nash-equilibrium proof.
