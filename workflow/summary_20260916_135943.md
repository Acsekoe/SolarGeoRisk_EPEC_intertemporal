# Selective best-response equilibrium search: current findings and next step

## Current methodological position

The paper's three converged Stage-1 profiles are stable endpoints of a
penalized/damped Gauss--Seidel procedure, but stability alone does not establish
equilibrium in the original zero-proximal economic game.  The operative local
acceptance test remains a common-frozen-profile audit in which every player's
unregularized unilateral deviation problem is solved and the largest normalized
profit gain is at most 1%.

The implemented selective rule is

\[
r_i(x)=\frac{[\Pi_i^{BR}(x_{-i})-\Pi_i(x)]_+}
              {\max\{|\Pi_i(x)|,1\}},
\]

with player \(i\) updated only when \(r_i(x)>0.01\).  An active player receives
the fixed damped update with `alpha = 0.30`; a player at or below 1% is left
unchanged for that turn and is reconsidered in the next sweep.  The update
decision is sequential, but acceptance is based only on the separate audit in
which all rivals are frozen at one common ending profile.

## Evidence obtained on 2026-09-16

The fixed nine-branch paper-profile experiment is preserved at:

`outputs/demand_calibration/paper_profile_3x3_a030_20260916_110336/`

It crossed the three manuscript player orders with three starts: the exact
Stage-1 paper profile, offers halfway to manufacturing cost, and offers reset
fully to manufacturing cost.  All branches used zero proximal penalties,
`alpha = 0.30`, no move cap, all-player updating, and at most 20 sweeps.
Only one branch passed: the full-cost start for order
`CH-AF-APAC-EU-ROW-US`, at sweep 6 with a frozen maximum gain of **0.6214%**.
The exact paper-profile branches ended with maximum gains of 4.0773%, 6.7158%,
and 7.0497%.  The halfway-to-cost branches reached best values of 1.4509%,
1.6059%, and 1.5365%, but did not pass.

Giving the three halfway branches ten additional all-player sweeps did not
produce an equilibrium and generally moved them farther away.  Those controls
are preserved at:

`outputs/demand_calibration/paper_profile_halfcost_extension_s21_s30_a030_20260916_124535/`

A post-hoc restart from the original halfway-to-cost sweep-20 profiles then
used the selective rule for sweeps 21--30:

`outputs/demand_calibration/paper_profile_halfcost_selective_s21_s30_a030_20260916_132207/`

The selective continuation improved the best result for every order relative
to the matched all-player extension.  It produced:

| Manuscript order | Best selective frozen gain | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| CH-AF-APAC-EU-ROW-US | **0.9641%** at sweep 24 | accepted |
| CH-AF-EU-US-ROW-APAC | 1.2134% at sweep 23 | no pass |
| CH-ROW-APAC-US-EU-AF | 1.3263% at sweep 25 | no pass |

For the accepted sweep-24 profile, all six audit solves succeeded and the
frozen gains were CH 0.1975%, AF 0.3396%, APAC 0.8337%, EU 0%, ROW 0.9641%,
and US 0%.  This is meaningful evidence that suppressing already-satisfied
players can prevent their small best-response movements from destabilizing
players that still have material deviations.  It is nevertheless exploratory:
the selective continuation was chosen only after the 20-sweep all-player
outcomes were known, and it started from halfway-to-cost rather than from the
unaltered paper profiles.

## Literature basis

The selective principle is not ad hoc.  Chien and Sinclair's
relative \(\varepsilon\)-Nash dynamics permits only deviations whose relative
improvement exceeds \(\varepsilon\).  Sagratella's Gauss--Southwell method, and
the explicit algorithm of Fabiani and Grammatico, retain a player's current
strategy when its best-response improvement is below an \(\varepsilon\)
threshold.  Related work calls the rule \(\varepsilon\)-satisficing.  Standard
electricity-market EPEC studies separately provide precedent for sequential
best-response diagonalization.

These papers motivate describing the method as a **gain-filtered, damped
Gauss--Seidel best-response scheme with an epsilon-satisficing activation
rule**.  Their convergence theorems do not automatically apply here because
they rely on congestion-game, potential-game, symmetry, monotonicity, or other
structures not established for this nonconvex EPEC.  The present one-start
audits also establish only computational local 1%-equilibrium candidates, not
global Nash equilibria.

Key references are:

- S. Chien and A. Sinclair, *Convergence to Approximate Nash Equilibria in
  Congestion Games*, Games and Economic Behavior 71(2), 2011,
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2009.05.004.
- S. Sagratella, *Algorithms for Generalized Potential Games with Mixed-Integer
  Variables*, Computational Optimization and Applications 68(3), 2017,
  https://doi.org/10.1007/s10589-017-9927-4.
- F. Fabiani and S. Grammatico, *Multi-Vehicle Automated Driving as a
  Generalized Mixed-Integer Potential Game*, IEEE Transactions on Intelligent
  Transportation Systems 21(3), 2020,
  https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2019.2901505.
- B. Yongacoglu, G. Arslan, and S. Yüksel, *Satisficing Paths and Independent
  Multiagent Reinforcement Learning in Stochastic Games*, SIAM Journal on
  Mathematics of Data Science 5(3), 2023,
  https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1515112.

## Recommended clean restart

If selective updating is to be part of the paper's stated algorithm, the search
should now start over under a protocol fixed before inspecting its outcomes:

1. Start separately from each of the **three exact converged paper profiles**,
   retaining all capacities and offer prices without a halfway-to-cost or
   full-cost transformation.
2. Use the corresponding manuscript player order, zero algorithmic proximal
   penalties, fixed `alpha = 0.30`, no move cap, and the 1% selective activation
   rule from the first released sweep.
3. Fix the sweep limit in advance, run the three branches with parallel workers,
   and preserve every sweep and failure.  Thirty sweeps is a natural first
   declared limit; a longer limit should be chosen before execution rather than
   added after seeing results.
4. Audit sweep 0 and every completed sweep at a common frozen profile.  Accept
   only when all six one-start deviation solves succeed and
   `max(relative gain) <= 1%`.
5. For a stronger comparison, run three otherwise identical all-player branches
   alongside the selective branches.  The earlier all-player trajectories are
   useful pilot evidence, but a newly declared matched comparison is cleaner.
6. Retain a separate multistart final diagnostic for any accepted profile and
   report it transparently; do not silently replace the declared one-start
   acceptance rule after observing the diagnostic.

This experiment would answer the missing question: whether the selective rule,
applied prospectively from the unaltered paper endpoints, can produce locally
audited 1%-equilibrium candidates.  A success would support the algorithm as a
reproducible epsilon-active-set refinement.  A failure would mean that the
successful cost-reset and halfway-to-cost results should remain exploratory
basin-search evidence rather than the main equilibrium justification.
