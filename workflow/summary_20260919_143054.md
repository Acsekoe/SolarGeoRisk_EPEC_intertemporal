# Workflow summary — Stage-1 continuation and equilibrium multiplicity

## Main finding

The three original terminal Stage-1 strategy profiles did **not** produce an
equilibrium after the algorithmic proximal penalties were removed. Each clean
Stage-1 endpoint was replayed directly from its workbook, with its saved
capacity-change path (`dK_net`) and bilateral offer prices (`p_offer`) left
unchanged at initialization. We then ran 15 additional Gauss--Seidel sweeps per
player order at damping 0.4 with proximal coefficients `(q,p,a,dk)=(0,0,0,0)`.

None of the three paths met the one-start, common-frozen-profile 1% equilibrium
criterion. The best audited maximum unilateral gains were:

- `ch-af-apac-eu-row-us`: 7.00% at sweep 15;
- `ch-af-eu-us-row-apac`: 4.46% at sweep 7;
- `ch-row-apac-us-eu-af`: 4.60% at sweep 15.

The trajectories oscillated rather than converging monotonically. Thus, simply
continuing the original Stage-1 profiles after removing the proximal penalty
did not yield an observable equilibrium within the 15-sweep experiment.

## Resulting methodological direction

Because the direct Stage-1 continuations failed, we used controlled
reinitializations **around the Stage-1 profiles** to explore additional basins
of attraction. The factorial varied the initial bilateral offer-price factor,
the weight on the Stage-1 capacity-change path, and the damping factor while
retaining the corrected demand data, terminal-state treatment, and 0.5 salvage
fraction. This basin exploration triggered multiple distinct observable
outcomes and produced 15 profiles satisfying the declared one-start local 1%
criterion across the three player orders.

These results support interpreting the model as having multiple observable
local equilibrium candidates whose discovery depends on initialization and
the resulting response basin, rather than obtaining a unique equilibrium by
directly removing the Stage-1 proximal penalty.

## Important caveat

The 15 profiles are one-start local equilibrium candidates. A subsequent
three-start robustness audit completed successfully but none remained below
1%. They therefore demonstrate computationally observable multiplicity under
the declared local search criterion, not globally certified Nash equilibria.
