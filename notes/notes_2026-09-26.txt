
[Planner benchmark - demand at Dmax / welfare gap is supply-side only]
In the global welfare planner and in all 27 accepted EPEC profiles, served demand equals D^max in every region and period (strategic prices, max 571 $/kW, never exceed regional WTP, which is the inverse-demand price at D^max). Gross consumer utility is therefore identical across planner and equilibria, so the 2.7% median global welfare gap (9,842.6 vs 10,114.7 bn USD) is purely a supply-side cost inefficiency (higher-cost production plus holding/investing in excess capacity). Everything else in the regional CS/PS shifts is a price transfer.

[Planner benchmark - EU/US exit is efficient, not caused by strategy]
The planner benchmark decommissions all EU, US, AF and ROW capacity by 2030, keeps China at 700 GW and APAC at 33 GW, and makes zero new investment; total capacity 2040 = 733 GW = global demand. China is the marginal supplier in every market, so planner prices equal Chinese cost + shipping (about 99-106 $/kW in 2040). Hence EU/US manufacturing exit is efficient under the calibrated cost gaps; strategic behavior explains the higher prices and the retained excess capacity, not the exit itself.

[Welfare - strategic pricing is not a pure transfer; over half of importers' loss is dissipated]
Across the 27 accepted profiles, the median net welfare loss of the four importing regions (EU, US, AF, ROW) is 454 bn USD, while the median joint gain of China and APAC is only 198 bn USD; the median global loss is 272 bn USD. Roughly 57% of what importers lose is therefore not captured by any exporter but destroyed. Because served demand equals Dmax everywhere, this deadweight loss is supply-side: extra capacity costs account for only ~34 bn USD (median), so most of it comes from higher-cost suppliers (domestic EU/US/APAC production) displacing cheaper Chinese supply. The capacity-only game loses just 0.1% (10,105 vs 10,115 bn USD), so nearly all of the 2.7% efficiency loss is attributable to strategic offer pricing, not strategic capacity choice.

[Multiplicity - China-dominated equilibria are the efficient ones]
Across the 27 profiles, global welfare rises with China's 2040 capacity share (Spearman 0.84), with total capacity (0.72 in 2040) and with cross-border trade (0.61), and falls with the average demand-weighted price (-0.71). China's own welfare co-moves positively with importers' welfare (0.71), while APAC's gain is unrelated to China's (-0.05) and weakly negatively related to importers' (-0.29). So the equilibria do not differ by who wins a fixed pie: in equilibria where China's low-cost supply dominates, prices are lower, importers lose less and China still earns more, whereas fragmented equilibria with more high-cost regional supply are worse for almost everyone except APAC. These are descriptive associations across accepted branches, not independent samples.
