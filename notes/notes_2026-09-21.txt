
[Paper consistency] Welfare accounting: the equilibrium-selection criterion (eq:global:welfare) subtracts a
separate capacity-cost term CC_{r,t}, but the ULP objective (eq:ulp:obj:revised) defines PS_{r,t} as *net*
producer surplus, already net of manufacturing, shipping, operating and capacity-investment costs, and adds a
50% salvage term instead. The selection criterion therefore double-counts investment cost relative to the
objective the players actually maximize, so the reported "welfare-maximizing" equilibrium is not ranked by the
same welfare measure the agents optimize. Fix is to mirror the ULP objective in the selection criterion.

[Basin search] Restart-grid pass rates are strongly asymmetric across the perturbation dimensions. In the
0.8 price-factor experiment only 2 of 12 branches passed the one-start 1% audit, both CH-first and both at
the full (k100) capacity path; every half-capacity (k050) start failed across all three update orders. In the
main pf100/pf120 grid, 8 of 16 branches passed, spread across both non-CH-first orders. This suggests the
low-capacity initialization lies outside the basin of any local equilibrium rather than selecting a different
one, so capacity-path scaling is a weak multiplicity lever compared with price-offer level and update order.
