[Methodology / verification consistency] The two equilibrium tests the paper describes (local unilateral test and single-offer undercutting) accept 15 of the 36 stage-2 branches, but the paper and letter report 14. The 15th profile (CH-first pf080_k100_a030) was removed by a joint US two-route withdrawal check that appears in neither document. Using a multi-route check for only one profile is inconsistent, because the analogous China multi-route re-entry check (2026-09-29 notes) gives gains of 1.2-3.5% in 12 of the 14 reported profiles. The paper should either describe every multi-route check with its results or report the profiles that pass the stated tests.

[Response R2.7 / scarcity rent is not a reason to stop investing] With EU domestic capacity binding and imports price-setting, the 23-44 $/kW gap between the EU price and c^man is the scarcity rent mu. That rent is the marginal value of additional capacity, so it argues for expansion, not against it. Back-of-envelope with mu = 30 $/kW: 1 GW added in 2025 costs 175 M$ and earns (30 - 8.8) x 5 x 2.469 = 262 M$ plus 59 M$ salvage, about +145 M$/GW. Use the rent to explain where the price gap goes (to EU/US producers), not why capacity stops where it does. That limit comes from the 1% tolerance (2026-10-05 expansion check).

[Interpretation / domestic capacity as a hedge] The game is open-loop (each region chooses its full 2025-2040 path while the others' paths are fixed), so a region's MPEC cannot value domestic capacity as a threat that disciplines exporters' offers. Any price-disciplining effect arises only through the exporters' best responses, and only if the capacity can cover the whole regional demand (ROW, not EU). Across the 14 equilibria, more EU/US capacity coincides with higher prices and larger CS losses (2026-10-01). The "hedge against strategically priced imports" wording in the abstract and conclusion is therefore not directly supported. A counterfactual with EU/US capacity fixed at zero would test it.

[Methodology / Stage-2 screening funnel behind the 14 equilibria] Stage 1 yields 3 converged candidates (CH-, AF-, EU-first), each restarted in 12 configurations, giving 36 runs. 28 pass the one-start local test within 15 unpenalized sweeps (7 no pass, 1 solver failure). Two accepted runs were then excluded outside the stated algorithm: ch-first pf100_k100_a040 for an EU 2035 price of 641 USD/kW, and eu-first pf120_k050_a040 by an extra deviation check whose script is not in the repo. Of the remaining 26, the undercutting screen rejects 11, and a route-withdrawal check rejects 1 more (ch-first pf080_k100_a030, US gain 1.16%). Only the local test and the undercutting screen are described in the paper, so the price-based exclusion and the withdrawal check are currently undocumented.

[Results / update order vs capacity split, 14 profiles] The 2040 capacity medians by Stage-1 update order are China 524 / EU 29 / US 30 GW for CH-first (5 profiles), 419 / 50 / 45 for EU-first (3), and 382 / 56 / 81 for AF-first (6). The model has simultaneous moves, so the paper must report this as an association with the numerical update sequence and not as first-mover or commitment behaviour.

[Equilibrium validity / scale of the 1% tolerance, 14 profiles] The relative gain is (J_dev - J_ref)/|J_ref|, where J is the region's full discounted upper-level objective including consumer surplus. For China (median J = 4,092 bn USD), 1% is about 41 bn USD, which is a quarter of the 172 bn spread in China's welfare across all 14 profiles. The largest detected gains are 0.50-0.98% in the local test (max 34.5 bn USD, China) and up to 0.99% (39 bn USD) in the undercutting screen. No profile passes local plus undercutting at 0.5%, and only 4 of 14 pass at 0.75%. With the greedy multi-route offer test included, 12 of 14 fail at 1% (China gains up to 3.49%, about 139 bn USD) and none passes at 0.75%.
