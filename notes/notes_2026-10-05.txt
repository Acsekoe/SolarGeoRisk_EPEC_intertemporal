[Equilibrium properties] Maximum demand D^max binds in every region, period and profile, and in the centralized benchmark. The highest clearing price across all 14 profiles (446 USD/kW, Africa 2025) is below the lowest willingness to pay (521 USD/kW, China), so beta > 0 everywhere. Served demand is therefore identical in all outcomes. Strategic pricing changes who produces and at what price, not how much is consumed. This explains why the global welfare loss is small (0.8-3.1%): there is no consumption distortion, only production misallocation and capacity costs. The demand elasticity |eta|=0.9 never matters in the equilibrium price range.

[Methodology / convergence] The 14 accepted profiles keep residual unilateral gains of 0.50-0.98% (median 0.76%), because stage 2 stops at the first sweep below eps_eq = 1%. The objectives are dominated by inframarginal consumer surplus, which is fixed because D^max binds. So 1% is large relative to the strategic stakes, e.g. APAC 0.98% of ~1,210 bn = ~12 bn USD against a median strategic gain of 130 bn. Report the profiles as eps-equilibria and give the residual gains explicitly.

[Equilibrium properties / overcapacity] With c^fix = 5% of c^inv per year and terminal salvage = 50% of c^inv, the salvage equals ten years of fixed costs (before discounting), so it offsets most of the cost of idle capacity. Rough estimate across the 14 profiles (others' strategies fixed): retiring China's avoidable idle capacity gains 0.00-0.04% of China's objective with salvage and 0.01-0.13% without. Salvage is therefore not the main driver. The overhang survives because China's fixed costs are tiny relative to its objective and the gain is far below eps_eq = 1%. The centralized benchmark has the same salvage term and still removes the idle capacity.

[Results / capacities] EU and Africa capacity is limited by the ramp-up bound g^exp, not by economics alone. The EU reaches 61.5 GW in 2040 in one profile against a ceiling of 62.05 GW (22 + 15 x 2.67), and Africa sits at 10.2-10.9 GW against a ceiling of 10.9 GW in all profiles. This is the direct answer to why EU prices stay above EU manufacturing cost without more domestic investment.

[Results / multiplicity] The 2040 China capacity figures quoted per update order (524 GW China-first, 382 GW importers-first) are medians within each sequence, not maxima. The China-first range is 434-590 GW and the af-first range is 342-544 GW.

[Model interpretation / R1.1] China has no formal PV export tariff, but its export VAT rebate is a de facto export-side wedge. MOF/STA Announcement No. 15 [2024] cut the PV rebate from 13% to 9% (effective 1 Dec 2024), and No. 2 [2026] abolished it (effective 1 Apr 2026). Since the standard VAT rate is 13%, an incomplete rebate acts as an export tax (Gourdon et al. 2022, Scand. J. Econ.). This gives a real-world counterpart to the strategic export offers above marginal cost in the ULP. Added as one sentence to the Discussion, citing only mof2026exportrebate.

[Sensitivity / R2.2 capacity-only game] If export offers are fixed at manufacturing cost, market power moves to capacity. China builds 616-624 GW by 2040 (benchmark 700 GW), so that together with APAC and Africa it falls just short of the 733 GW of demand. A higher-cost EU producer then stays marginal and sets a nearly uniform price of 143-175 $/kW (benchmark 90-106). Global welfare is only about 0.1% below the benchmark (strategic median: 1.7%), because production still concentrates at the cheapest locations. So the welfare loss in the main game comes mainly from regionally dispersed, higher-cost production, not from the price premium itself.

[Equilibrium tally / 36 Stage-2 branches] The two locally accepted profiles that were never undercut-screened both fail the same screen (outputs/undercut_screen_unscreened_20261005). These are CH-first pf100_k100_a040, previously dropped only for its 641 USD/kW EU price (APAC->EU 2035, +3.3%), and EU-first pf120_k050_a040 (APAC->EU 2030, +6.4%). The correct breakdown of the 36 branches is: 8 fail the local test within the iteration limit (7 no-pass, 1 solver failure), 13 fail the single-offer undercutting test, 15 pass both. One of the 15 (CH-first pf080_k100_a030) is excluded through the joint US two-route withdrawal check (+1.16%). The letter's earlier "9 / 12" split was off by one in each class.

[Equilibrium tolerance / EU-US investment, R2.7] Further domestic capacity expansion would still pay in the 14 reported profiles (outputs/eu_us_expansion_check_20261005, other strategies fixed). Moving EU net capacity changes in 2025-2035 towards g^exp raises the EU objective in 14/14 profiles, by 0.01-0.50% (median 0.21%, max 9.9 bn USD), with the optimum at the bound in 12/14. For the US, gains are 0.2-0.5% in 11/14 profiles (max 3.3 bn), always at small expansions (25-50% of the gap). All gains are below the 1% tolerance eps_eq, because regional welfare is dominated by consumer surplus (EU CS ~1,940 bn vs PS ~24 bn). The 1% test therefore cannot resolve producer-side capacity decisions of the importing regions, and the reported EU/US capacities are, if anything, lower bounds. This is the honest answer to "what stops the EU and US from investing".
