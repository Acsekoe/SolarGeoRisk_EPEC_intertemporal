2026-09-18 -- Demand calibration: elasticity enters only as a slope scaler
The full-demand anchor (D^max_{i,t}, WTP_i) is invariant to the assumed
elasticity, because a_i = WTP_i(1+1/|eps|) and b_{i,t} = WTP_i/(|eps| D^max_{i,t})
are both derived by imposing the point elasticity at that same anchor. Hence
|eps| does not change the price at which full demand is served; it only rescales
b_{i,t} and the markup range (a_i - WTP_i) by 1/|eps| -- the literature range
[0.65, 1.20] corresponds to slopes of 1.385x and 0.75x the base case |eps|=0.90.
This is the cleanest defence of using a single midpoint elasticity in the paper
without running an elasticity sensitivity: the calibration target is the WTP
anchor, not the slope itself.

2026-09-18 -- Direct vs inverse slope: two different b's in the calibration memo
demand_model.tex Section 1 reports b_{2025,i} = |eps| D^max/WTP (units GW/$, the
DIRECT demand slope, e.g. ch = 0.480), while the model and the paper use the
INVERSE slope b_{i,t} = WTP/(|eps| D^max) (units $/kW/GW, ch = 1.788 at
D^max = 324). The two are reciprocals, so the memo's table and the model input are
consistent, but the same symbol b denotes different objects. Only the inverse slope
should ever appear in the paper, since lambda = a - b x^dem.

2026-09-18 -- Africa's slope is two orders of magnitude above China's
With WTP_af = 1135.6 $/kW and D^max_af,2025 = 10 GW, b_af,2025 = 126.2 $/kW/GW
versus b_ch,2025 = 1.79. Small high-WTP markets therefore get very steep inverse
demand, so even a few GW of unserved Africa demand moves the African price
strongly. Worth keeping in mind when interpreting regional price spreads: part of
the African price level is a calibration artefact of the small market size, not of
strategic behaviour.

2026-09-18 -- Pitfall: the elasticity denominator is WTP, not a
When writing the calibration formula, note that the point elasticity is imposed at
the anchor (D^max, WTP), so b = WTP/(|eps| D^max). Using a in place of WTP gives a
slope too steep by the factor a/WTP = 1 + 1/|eps| (2.111 at |eps| = 0.90) and
silently realises an elasticity of -|eps| WTP/a = -0.43 instead of -0.90. WTP is the
primitive; a = WTP(1 + 1/|eps|) is derived from the anchor plus the slope.

2026-09-18 -- The demand cap binds in every market-year: results are eps-invariant
In the selected equilibrium (15_equilibria, order ch-af-apac-eu-row-us, pf100_k050_a030,
sweep_014) served demand equals D^max in all 6 regions x 4 periods (gap < 1e-7 GW), and
every clearing price lies at 16-53% of the regional WTP (highest: us 2025 at 0.53, lowest
ch 2040 at 0.17). The effective demand curve is kinked -- vertical at D^max for
lambda <= WTP, elastic only above WTP -- so the equilibrium sits entirely on the vertical
segment. Consequence: b_{i,t}, and therefore the elasticity assumption, has no effect on
equilibrium prices, quantities, capacities or trade flows. Prices are disciplined by rival
offers and the offer cap, not by demand response.

2026-09-18 -- eps enters welfare only as an exogenous constant that cancels in differences
With x^dem = D^max the consumer surplus collapses to
CS = WTP*D^max*(1 + 1/(2|eps|)) - lambda*D^max, so the eps-dependent part
WTP*D^max/(2|eps|) is purely exogenous. It is identical in the EPEC and planner runs and
cancels exactly in the EPEC-minus-planner welfare differences reported in the paper. Its
magnitude is large (China 2025 alone: 93.9 bn$ at |eps|=0.9, 130.0 bn$ at 0.65, 70.4 bn$
at 1.2), which is a strong argument for reporting welfare *differences* rather than levels:
the level carries an arbitrary elasticity-dependent constant.
