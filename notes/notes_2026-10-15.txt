[Results / equilibrium properties] Submitted vs. revised comparison as evidence for the multiplicity argument
Comparing the submitted single (unverified) profile with the 14 verified local equilibria shows which findings are robust: the price premium in net-importing regions and the welfare transfer from importing consumers to exporting producers survive (global welfare loss 1.05% before vs. 0.8-3.1% now). The capacity findings do not: EU/US exit by 2030 reversed to median growth (EU 22->47 GW, US 42->52 GW), and the "overcapacity as strategic buffer" reading collapsed because retiring China's idle capacity changes its objective by <0.1%. This is a concrete argument for the methodological conclusion that single-equilibrium EPEC studies can report price/welfare results reliably but capacity allocations only with a verified equilibrium set.

[Results / prices] The global average gap understates the price premium in importing regions
The demand-weighted global gap to the centralized benchmark (53 -> 42 USD/kW, 2025-2040) is diluted by China, whose price tracks the benchmark. In the net-importing regions the median gap is 133-167 USD/kW in 2025 and 82-109 USD/kW in 2040 (ROW 82, US 92, EU 96, AF 109), i.e. prices about 1.8-2.1 times the benchmark. Even the lowest of the 14 equilibria stays at least 66 USD/kW above the benchmark in every importing region in 2040. These regional numbers are not yet stated in Section IV-C and would make the price result more tangible.
