[Welfare / China vs APAC gains, 14 profiles, PS net of capacity cost] The median gains are similar (China 119, APAC 130 bn USD) but come through different channels. China: CS +2.5, PS +116, because its capacity never binds and its domestic offer is at cost. APAC: PS +206, CS -72, because its capacity binds and the domestic price carries the export opportunity cost, so about 36% of APAC's PS gain is paid by its own consumers. The similarity holds only at the median. China's gain ranges 7.5-180 bn and exceeds APAC's in 5/14 profiles (4 of 5 CH-first). China and APAC gains are weakly negatively related (Spearman -0.35).
[Welfare / China gains from volume, APAC from price] Across the 14 profiles (horizon-weighted, PS net of capacity cost), China's PS change correlates with its own export volume (Spearman 0.95) and falls with the average price (-0.64). APAC's PS change correlates with the average price (0.79) and is unrelated to China's exports (-0.29), because APAC's exports are capacity-bound (95-100% utilisation), so its PS is a rent on a fixed volume. China's export volume cleanly separates the profiles. China's welfare gain exceeds APAC's in all 5 profiles where China exports 75-113 GW/yr (4 CH-first plus EU-first pf120_k100_a040) and falls short in all 9 where it exports 0-61 GW/yr. Update order alone does not separate them (CH-first pf120_k100_a040 exports 52 GW and China gains less than APAC).
[Welfare / cross-equilibrium links, 14 profiles, verified] Global welfare change vs the four importers' 2040 domestic output: Spearman -0.89 (the earlier -0.92 note was on a different set; median 2040 importer output 122.6 GW). China's welfare co-moves with the importers' (0.77), APAC's moves against them (-0.72). Median importer CS loss 460 bn, PS gain 36 bn (net -427), global loss 174 bn. APAC is the only region that prefers equilibria in which China withdraws from exports.
[Welfare / intra-regional vs cross-border transfers, 14 profiles] Route-level operating margins (destination price minus cost minus shipping, discounted 2%, 5-year periods) split PS by market. China's median export margin (116.8 bn) equals its net PS gain (116.4), and its domestic margin is about 2 bn, so China's entire PS gain comes from foreign consumers. APAC earns a median 70 bn on domestic sales and 149 bn on exports. Its domestic margin equals 93-99% of its CS loss in 13/14 profiles (72% in one), so APAC's CS loss is almost fully an intra-regional transfer to APAC producers via the binding-capacity rent, and APAC's net welfare gain comes from export rents paid by foreign consumers. About one third of APAC's PS gain is this domestic transfer. The importers' small PS gains (median 36 bn joint) are likewise domestic scarcity rents paid by their own consumers.
[Welfare / domestic EU-US capacity does not protect EU-US consumers, 14 profiles] EU+US 2040 capacity is almost a mirror of China's exports (Spearman -0.94). Tercile medians by EU+US horizon output (low/mid/high = 22/64/89 GW): China exports 95/56/10 GW, average price 160/164/171 USD/kW, China PS +164/+126/+39, APAC PS +194/+202/+223, EU CS -153/-166/-226 and PS +7/+11/+34, US CS -109/-129/-141 and PS +1/+14/+33, global welfare -108/-162/-295 bn. Equilibria with more EU/US domestic capacity are those where China withdraws. EU/US producers then earn scarcity rents, but consumers lose more, so EU welfare falls with its capacity (rho -0.72), while US welfare is roughly unaffected (rho -0.14) because US PS offsets the CS loss. Associations, not causal: domestic capacity fills the gap left by China rather than raising prices. This contradicts reading the importers' domestic capacity as protective self-sufficiency.
