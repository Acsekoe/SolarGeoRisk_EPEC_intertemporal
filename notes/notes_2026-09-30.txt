
[Convergence / Stage 1 endpoints] Of the 7 penalized Stage-1 update orders, 3 meet the 1% movement criterion (CH-first at sweep 26, AF-first and EU-first at sweep 33 after continuation). The AF-first and EU-first trajectories differ in early sweeps but end at the same profile (total capacity 7120.50 vs 7120.53 GW summed over periods, identical final movement 0.19%). Stage 1 therefore yields 2 distinct candidate endpoints, not 3. The 14 equilibria differ because of the Stage-2 restarts (offer factor, capacity weight, damping), not because of Stage-1 diversity. Source: _MOVE/new_equilibria/penalized_profiles/*/iters.

[Convergence / Stage 2 acceptance] In the unpenalized Stage 2 the strategy movement never falls below 1% for the 14 retained profiles (final sweep 1.3-10%). Acceptance comes from the unilateral-gain test instead. The frozen-profile one-start gain falls from 3-7% at restart (peaks up to 16%) to 0.5-0.98% after 4-11 sweeps, often non-monotonically. This supports the paper's argument that non-marginal offers are weakly identified (strategies keep moving while payoffs do not). It also motivates the Stage-1 proximal term and justifies testing payoffs rather than strategies in Stage 2.

[Idle capacity is nearly free to hold, so the 1% test cannot pin its level] From the objective in model_main.py (5-year weights, 2% discounting, salvage 0.5*c_inv on K_2045), keeping 1 GW idle from 2030 to the end costs China 2.5*5*(0.906+0.820+0.743) = 30.9 M$ in fixed costs and returns 25*0.673 = 16.8 M$ salvage, a net 14 M$/GW. For China's median 118 GW of idle 2040 capacity this is about 1.7 bn USD, or 0.04% of its objective (about 4,084 bn), some 25 times below the 1% tolerance (about 41 bn). For ROW the net cost is about 36 M$/GW, so 34 GW cost about 1.2 bn (0.08%). Idle capacity carries mu = 0 and has no marginal market value, the model has simultaneous moves and therefore no commitment or deterrence channel, and holding it is almost costless. Its level is therefore not identified by the equilibrium test and should not be framed as a strategic buffer.

[Methodology / why IPOPT misses undercutting] With the relaxed complementarity gamma*x <= eps_comp, a route whose landed offer lies above the clearing price has gamma > 0 and flow x <= eps/gamma. Lowering that offer raises the admissible flow by only about eps/gamma^2 per unit, so the objective sensitivity is roughly margin*eps/gamma^2, which is numerically zero at eps = 1e-3 and gamma of tens of USD/kW. Entry is a change of active set (a combinatorial move), which a local interior-point method cannot see. Reliable detection needs either exact complementarity with binaries (global MIQCP best response) or starting points placed inside the entry regime. The installed GAMSPy license solves nonconvex MIQCP globally with GUROBI and SCIP (tested 2026-09-30 on a toy model only, size limits not yet checked).

[China and APAC divide rather than share markets, even in the two robust profiles] In the two CH-first k100_a030 profiles (the only ones passing the multi-route re-entry check), China and APAC export similar totals (2040 China 111 GW vs APAC 119-135 GW). They rarely serve the same market, though. Both deliver more than 1 GW in only 4 of 16 importer-market years, which equals the maximum among the other 12 profiles. In Eq 2 in 2040, China supplies 100% of ROW and 61% of Africa, while APAC supplies 67% of the EU and 71% of the US. The assignment of markets also flips between periods (e.g. ROW goes China, APAC, China, China, and the US goes APAC, China, APAC, APAC). The robust result is an equal aggregate export role with geographic market division, consistent with Bertrand-type offers where each market goes to the lowest landed offer. It is not head-to-head competition within markets.

[Capacity-price slope is China's export role, not idle capacity] Served demand equals Dmax everywhere, so horizon-weighted global output is identical in all 14 equilibria (671.7 GW). Differences in total capacity are therefore purely differences in idle capacity, and the scatter slope (-10 USD/kW per 100 GW) is formally a relation between idle capacity and price (Spearman rho -0.64 for both). Idle capacity moves with China's exports (rho 0.87). Controlling for China's exports, idle capacity is unrelated to price (partial rank rho +0.10), whereas China's exports stay related to price when idle capacity is controlled for (partial -0.55). Equilibria in which China exports more keep more capacity and clear at lower prices because China's landed offers set the prices, not because idle capacity disciplines them. The paper's "more capacity tends to result in lower prices" should be reworded as an association. Horizon weights are 1/6, 1/3, 1/3, 1/6.

[Domestic prices of China and APAC follow the capacity dual] Both domestic offers are fixed at manufacturing cost, so lambda = c + mu at home. China's capacity never binds (42/42 observations 2030-2040), so its price stays at cost (+0.3 USD/kW). APAC's capacity binds in 38/42 observations, with a price-cost gap of 2.9-111 USD/kW (median 46), and the gap falls to 0.1 in the 4 non-binding cases. APAC consumers thus pay the export opportunity cost, which explains APAC's CS loss alongside its PS gain. This is not a rationale for China's idle capacity. With demand fixed at Dmax, a higher domestic price only moves surplus between Chinese consumers and producers and leaves China's objective unchanged (as the paper states for eq. price:domestic). Keeping China's CS at the benchmark is therefore a consequence of non-binding capacity, not a purpose.
