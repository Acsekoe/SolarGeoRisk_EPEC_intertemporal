[Calibration / expansion limits are not historical maxima] The g_exp limits (CH 27.4, EU 2.67, US 5.33, APAC 12.67, AF 0.5, ROW 4.33 GW/yr; applied as 5 x g_exp per 5-year period) are far below the peak module-capacity additions actually observed: IEA reports ~330 GW of new module plants worldwide in 2023 (>90% in China), the US added ~25 GW in 2024 (SEIA: 11 GW in Q1, 9.3 GW in Q3), and India added 25 GW (2024) and 81 GW (2025). The paper therefore cannot describe them as "historically highest observed". This matters because the EU/US best deviations often go up to the expansion limit and Africa sits at its bound in 54-69% of profiles (2026-09-27/28 notes), so EU/US/AF capacity outcomes are partly driven by these limits.

[Calibration / shipping costs are in a plausible range] Off-diagonal c_ship spans 4.5-24.7 USD/kW (median 13.7). China-EU (16.1 USD/kW) is about 9% of the delivered cost at c_man(CH) = 163, close to the ~10% long-run transport-cost share and below the 14.7-15.8% current share that Mittag et al. (EPJ PV 15, 40, 2024) estimate for China-Germany module shipments. The range lies between pre-pandemic freight (~0.004-0.006 EUR/W) and the 2021 peak (~0.05-0.06 EUR/W).

[Calibration / expansion limits match the IEA 2035 outlook] The g_exp values are best justified as sustained build-out rates from IEA ETP 2024, not as historical peaks. Global module capacity grows 1,155 -> 1,546 GW (STEPS, 2023-2035), i.e. +32.6 GW/yr, and China's 27.4 GW/yr is 84% of that, matching IEA Renewables 2023 (China takes ~85% of module capacity expansion to 2028). The US limit gives at most 95 GW in 2035 versus ~90 GW in the STEPS (announced projects: 75 GW by 2030, i.e. ~5 GW/yr). The EU limit gives at most 35 GW in 2030, close to 40% of the model's 2030 EU demand (NZIA benchmark, 36 GW). The 2023 boom (~500 GW added, ~440 GW in China, at ~50% utilisation; IEA ACTM 2024) is the counter-argument against using peaks.

[Calibration / initial capacities vs IEA] Model EU capacity (22 GW) is about twice IEA's "<1% of global" (~11 GW, ETP 2024), because 2024 and 2025 source years are mixed (accepted by the authors). The AF region includes the Middle East, so its 3.4 GW matches Sinovoltaics' Middle East + Africa module nameplate (Sept 2025). Africa alone would be ~0.9 GW (Ember). Because AF is defined as MEA, the 0.5 GW/yr expansion limit is conservative next to the MEA pipeline of up to 62 GW of modules by 2030 (Sinovoltaics).

[Results / why domestic capacity cannot restore benchmark prices] Comparing the learning-adjusted manufacturing cost c_man_t with the 2% centralized benchmark prices shows that EU (299 -> 159 USD/kW, 2025-2040), US (321 -> 170) and ROW (353 -> 187) costs exceed their benchmark prices (179 -> 106, 174 -> 101, 178 -> 104) in every period. More EU capacity therefore could not bring EU prices down to the benchmark, so the EU is cost-limited as well as capacity-limited. Africa is the only importer at cost parity (181 -> 96 vs 174 -> 100), so its gap to the benchmark comes from capacity alone (about 11 GW at most against 28 GW of demand in 2040).
